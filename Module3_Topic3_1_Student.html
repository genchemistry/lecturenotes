The Mole and Avogadro Constant

Learning Objective: Define the mole and use the exact Avogadro constant as the mole–entity relationship

Everyday counting

1 dozen = 12 objects

Chemical counting

1 mol = 6.02214076 × 1023 specified entities

One mole contains exactly 6.02214076 × 1023 elementary entities.
The entities may be atoms, molecules, formula units, or ions.
Why the number is so large: atoms and molecules are extremely small, so measurable samples contain extraordinarily many representative particles.
1 mol
↔
6.022 × 1023 entities

Examples: 1 mol Al = 6.022 × 10²³ Al atoms; 1 mol CO₂ = 6.022 × 10²³ CO₂ molecules; 1 mol NaCl = 6.022 × 10²³ NaCl formula units.
Important distinction: One CO₂ molecule contains three total atoms, but one mole of CO₂ contains 6.022 × 10²³ CO₂ molecules—not three times as many molecules.

Molar Mass — The Grams-per-Mole Bridge

Learning Objective: Define molar mass and distinguish g/mol from amu.

Particle-Level Mass

One NO₂ molecule has a molecular mass of approximately 46.01 amu.

Mole-Level Mass

One mole of NO₂ molecules has a molar mass of 46.01 g/mol.

Molar mass = mass in grams of 1 mole of a substance
FormulaAtomic-Mass SumMolar Mass
NO₂14.01 + 2(16.00)46.01 g/mol
C₂H₆2(12.01) + 6(1.008)30.07 g/mol
ACS misconception check: amu describes the mass of one particle; g/mol describes the mass of one mole. The numerical values correspond, but the units and scales do not.

Guided Example B — Ethane Molecules to Grams

Learning Objective: Complete a two-step molecules-to-grams conversion through moles.

Problem

Convert 9.03 × 10²³ C₂H₆ molecules to grams of C₂H₆.

Plan

molecules → moles → grams

Molar mass of C₂H₆ = 30.07 g/mol

9.03 × 10²³ C₂H₆ molecules × 1 mol C₂H₆ 6.022 × 10²³ C₂H₆ molecules × 30.07 g C₂H₆ 1 mol C₂H₆
C₂H₆ molecules cancel first; mol C₂H₆ cancels next; g C₂H₆ remains
9.03 × 10²³ C₂H₆ molecules = [ _______ ]

Intermediate Check

9.03 × 10²³ molecules is 1.50 mol C₂H₆.

Final Check

1.50 mol × about 30 g/mol should be about 45 g.

Conversion Decision Check — Common Errors

Learning Objective: Diagnose formula-reading, particle-label, and conversion-factor errors before calculation
Formula error
Ignoring an outside subscript or hydrate coefficient.
Fix
Expand the formula first; then calculate mass.
Particle error
Calling NaCl a molecule or multiplying molecule count by total atoms.
Fix
Name atoms, molecules, formula units, or ions explicitly.
Conversion error
Going directly from grams to particles with only Avogadro number.
Fix
grams ↔ moles by molar mass; moles ↔ particles by Avogadro constant.
