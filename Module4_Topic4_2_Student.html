Solubility Chart — The Decision Tool

Learning Objective: Use the course solubility chart to decide whether an ionic compound is aqueous or solid.
Formal solubility-chart moment: In T4.1, AgCl was labeled insoluble for you. From here, the chart tells us.

Always / Generally Soluble

  • Group 1 cations: Li⁺, Na⁺, K⁺, Rb⁺, Cs⁺
  • NH₄⁺ ammonium salts
  • NO₃⁻ nitrate salts
  • CH₃COO⁻ acetate salts

Usually Soluble, Check Exceptions

  • Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻ except with Ag⁺, Pb²⁺, Hg₂²⁺
  • SO₄²⁻ except with Ba²⁺, Sr²⁺, Pb²⁺; Ca²⁺ is often low-solubility depending on chart

Usually Insoluble

  • CO₃²⁻ carbonate
  • PO₄³⁻ phosphate
  • CrO₄²⁻ chromate
  • OH⁻ hydroxide
  • S²⁻ sulfide

Insoluble-Rule Exceptions

  • Often soluble with Group 1 or NH₄⁺
  • Hydroxide and sulfide exceptions depend on the course chart
  • Use the course solubility chart as final authority
How to read it: Find the anion family first, then check whether the cation is an exception.

Course pattern: In this course, precipitation reactions are predicted using the double-displacement pattern.
Outside + outside
A and D pair to form AD
Inside + inside
B and C pair; write cation first, so CB
AB + CD → AD + CB

Molecular Equation Prediction — KI + Pb(NO₃)₂

Learning Objective: Write and balance a molecular equation for a precipitation reaction.
KI(aq) + Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) → PbI₂(s) + KNO₃(aq)
State symbols: PbI₂ is the precipitate; KNO₃ remains dissolved.
Balance iodide and nitrate groups:
Pb(NO₃)₂ has2 nitrate groups→ need2KNO₃
PbI₂ has2 iodide ions→ need2KI
2KI(aq) + Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) → PbI₂(s) + 2KNO₃(aq)
