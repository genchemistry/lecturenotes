Recall from T2.5

Many d-block metals can form more than one positive ion.
Main-group variable-charge metals to remember:
Sn²⁺ / Sn⁴⁺   and   Pb²⁺ / Pb⁴⁺
Do not overgeneralize: common fixed-charge metals in the d-block include Ag⁺, Zn²⁺, and Cd²⁺. These do not need Roman numerals in their compound names.
Variable-charge naming rule: determine the charge on one metal cation, then place that charge as a Roman numeral after the metal name. The Roman numeral is a charge, not an atom count.

Learning Objective: Write formulas by choosing the correct logic for ionic versus molecular names.

di = 2; tetra = 4

Recall from T2.6: Magic CrossIonic compounds only

Cross charge magnitudes, reduce if needed, and check total charge = 0.

Molecular rule

Do not use Magic Cross.

Prefixes already tell you the atom counts.

dinitrogen tetroxide → N₂O₄

Do not reduce N₂O₄ to NO₂.

Ionic check

calcium oxide

Ca²⁺ + O²⁻ → Ca₂O₂ → CaO

Reduce to the lowest whole-number ion ratio.

Polyatomic ionic check

calcium nitrate

Ca²⁺ + NO₃⁻ → Ca(NO₃)₂

Keep the polyatomic ion intact; use parentheses when repeated.

System-selection checkpoint: First decide ionic or molecular. Magic Cross belongs only to ionic formula writing. Roman numeral → determine ionic charge. Greek prefix → use molecular atom count.
