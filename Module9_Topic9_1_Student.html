Why Do Atoms Form Bonds?

Learning Objective: Connect chemical bonding to a decrease in potential energy.

A stable bonded arrangement has lower potential energy than the separated atoms.

A bond forms when the combined attractions outweigh the energetic costs at a suitable internuclear separation.

Forming a stable bond releases energy. Breaking that bond requires [ _______ ].

The octet rule is a useful electron-counting pattern. It is not the physical cause of bonding.

Polar Bonds: Follow the Electron Density

Learning Objective: Assign partial charges and interpret a chemical dipole arrow.

Cl attracts the shared electrons more strongly: Cl is [ _______ ] and H is [ _______ ].

Blue δ+ = electron-poor / electropositive end.
Red δ− = electron-rich / electronegative end.
The chemistry dipole arrow points toward the negative end; its crossed positive tail is blue. Atom colors and electron-origin colors have separate legends.
Partial charges are not full ionic charges. Formal charge is a different bookkeeping system introduced later.

Percent Ionic Character: A Dipole Comparison

Learning Objective: Interpret an observed dipole relative to an ideal fully ionic diatomic model.
SymbolRead it asMeaning
μmu — Greek letterMagnitude of the dipole moment: how much charge separation the bond produces.
eelementary chargeMagnitude of one full elementary charge, approximately 1.602 × 10−19 C.
rrInternuclear separation: the distance between the two nuclei.
For two equal and opposite charges, μ = q × r. The fully ionic reference sets q = e. A larger charge separation or a longer distance gives a larger dipole magnitude.
% ionic character = μobservedμionic × 100%
μobserved: the measured dipole magnitude.
μionic = e × r: the calculated reference for full charge separation at the same distance.
Use the same dipole units in the numerator and denominator.
Illustrative data: 1.2 D6.0 D × 100% = [ _______ ]
D is the debye, a dipole-moment unit. The ratio is unitless. This comparison does not mean a bond switches between two kinds of bonding.
