Worked Example — Find Molarity from Mass and Volume

Learning Objective: Convert solute mass to moles, then divide by liters of solution.

Problem

What is the molarity of a solution containing 25.5 g KBr dissolved to make 1.75 L of solution?

25.5 g KBr
given mass
mol KBr
use molar mass
1.75 L solution
given final volume
M
moles over liters
Plan first: grams KBr → moles KBr → divide by liters of solution → molarity.

Step 1 — grams → moles

Molar mass KBr = 39.10 + 79.90 = 119.00 g per mol
25.5 g KBr × 1 mol KBr119.00 g KBr = 0.214 mol KBr

Step 2 — moles over liters

M = 0.214 mol KBr1.75 L solution
M = 0.122 M KBr
Answer: 0.122 M KBr

Preparing a Solution from a Solid

Learning Objective: Calculate the solute mass needed, then describe the correct volumetric-flask preparation.

Problem

How many grams of NaNO₃ are needed to prepare 100.0 mL of 1.30 × 10⁻² M NaNO₃?

100.0 mL
0.1000 L
mol NaNO₃
g NaNO₃
prepare solution

Calculation

0.1000 L × 1.30 × 10⁻² mol1 L = 1.30 × 10⁻³ mol NaNO₃
Molar mass NaNO₃ = 85.00 g per mol
1.30 × 10⁻³ mol × 85.00 g1 mol = 0.111 g NaNO₃

Volumetric flask

Bring the final solution to the calibration line.

Correct preparation sequence

1. Weigh 0.111 g NaNO₃.
2. Transfer it to the volumetric flask.
3. Add some water and dissolve completely.
4. Add water until the bottom of the meniscus reaches the calibration line.
5. Stopper and mix thoroughly.
Do not add 100.0 mL of water. Make the final solution volume 100.0 mL.

Worked Solution Stoichiometry — HNO₃ + Na₂CO₃

Learning Objective: Find the volume of one reacting solution from the volume and molarity of another.

Problem and balanced equation

What volume of 0.150 M HNO₃ reacts completely with 35.7 mL of 0.108 M Na₂CO₃?

Na₂CO₃(aq) + 2HNO₃(aq) → 2NaNO₃(aq) + CO₂(g) + H₂O(l)

Step 1 — Na₂CO₃ solution → mol HNO₃

35.7 mL × 1 L1000 mL = 0.0357 L
0.0357 L × 0.108 mol Na₂CO₃1 L = 0.00386 mol Na₂CO₃
0.00386 mol Na₂CO₃ × 2 mol HNO₃1 mol Na₂CO₃ = 0.00771 mol HNO₃

Step 2 — mol HNO₃ → solution volume

0.00771 mol HNO₃ × 1 L0.150 mol HNO₃ = 0.0514 L
0.0514 L × 1000 mL1 L = 51.4 mL HNO₃
Answer: 51.4 mL of 0.150 M HNO₃

Reverse Solution Stoichiometry — Precipitate Mass → Solution Volume

Learning Objective: Start from a product mass and work backward to the volume of a reacting solution.

Problem and equation

What volume of 0.478 M NaI reacted if 123.6 g PbI₂ formed?

Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2NaI(aq) → PbI₂(s) + 2NaNO₃(aq)
Direction: g PbI₂ → mol PbI₂ → mol NaI → L NaI solution.

Step 1 — product mass → reactant moles

123.6 g PbI₂ × 1 mol PbI₂461.0 g PbI₂ = 0.2681 mol PbI₂
0.2681 mol PbI₂ × 2 mol NaI1 mol PbI₂ = 0.5362 mol NaI

Step 2 — NaI moles → solution volume

0.5362 mol NaI × 1 L solution0.478 mol NaI = 1.12 L NaI solution
Answer: 1.12 L of 0.478 M NaI

EXAM REVIEW — Molarity & Solution Stoichiometry

Use these checks before calculating.
Error 1: mL used directly with M
M = molL, so convert mL → L first.
Error 2: no balanced equation
Without coefficients, there is no valid mole ratio.
Error 3: assuming 1:1
Use the actual coefficients: e.g., Na₂CO₃ : HNO₃ = 1 : 2.
Error 4: wrong solution volume
For preparation, use final solution volume — not water added.
Equation first → units second → arithmetic last.
Fast unit check: every unit should cancel except the unit requested in the question.

ACS-Style Practice — Easy → Medium → Hard

Learning Objective: Apply molarity and solution stoichiometry in multiple-choice problems with common distractors.

Easy

How many moles of NaCl are present in 50.0 mL of 0.200 M NaCl?

A. 0.0100 mol
B. 0.0400 mol
C. 10.0 mol
D. 100 mol
Answer: A
50.0 mL = 0.0500 L, then 0.0500 L × 0.200 mol/L = 0.0100 mol.

Medium

What volume of 0.250 M HCl contains 0.0500 mol HCl?

A. 20.0 mL
B. 50.0 mL
C. 200 mL
D. 500 mL
Answer: C
V = moles / molarity = 0.0500 mol / 0.250 mol/L = 0.200 L = 200 mL.

Hard

For H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O, what volume of 0.300 M NaOH is required to react completely with 25.0 mL of 0.150 M H₂SO₄?

A. 12.5 mL
B. 25.0 mL
C. 37.5 mL
D. 50.0 mL
Answer: B
25.0 mL = 0.0250 L → 0.00375 mol H₂SO₄ → 0.00750 mol NaOH → 0.0250 L = 25.0 mL NaOH.
