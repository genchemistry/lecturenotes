Learning Objective: Interpret elements, subscripts, parentheses, and charge in chemical formulas.

A chemical formula identifies the elements present and their relative numbers.

A subscript applies to the symbol or grouped unit immediately before it.

Ca(NO₃)₂

parentheses keep NO₃ together
outside 2 applies to the whole NO₃ unit

Ionic Formula Roadmap — Principle First

Learning Objective: Use charge neutrality as the principle behind ionic formulas and prepare to use the Magic Cross shortcut correctly.
1Confirm that the compound is ionic.
2Write the cation and anion symbols with their charges.
3Choose the smallest ion ratio that makes total charge [_______].
4Write that ratio as subscripts and reduce if needed.
5Use parentheses when more than one polyatomic ion is needed.
6Remove ion charges from the final neutral formula and check.
Ionic compounds only Magic Cross is a shortcut for Steps 2–4. It does not replace the idea that the final compound must be electrically neutral.

Magic Cross Shortcut — Ionic Compounds Only

Learning Objective: Use the Magic Cross shortcut to write a neutral binary ionic formula, then verify the result by charge balance.
Use only after you know it is ionic
Example: aluminum oxide → Al3+ and O2−. Cross only the charge magnitudes. Do not carry the + or − signs into the subscripts.
1. Al has charge magnitude 3; O has charge magnitude 2.
2. Cross 3 to oxygen and 2 to aluminum.
3. Write Al₂O₃.
4. Check: 2(+3) + 3(−2) = 0.
Do not cross the signs. The signs tell you cation versus anion; the numbers become subscripts.

Reduce 2:2 to the lowest whole-number ratio, 1:1.

Magic Cross with a Polyatomic Ion

Learning Objective: Apply Magic Cross to an ionic formula containing a polyatomic ion and use parentheses correctly.
Still ionic only Treat a polyatomic ion as one intact charged unit. Example: calcium nitrate → Ca2+ and NO3−.
Cross the 2 from Ca²⁺ to nitrate.
Cross the 1 from NO₃⁻ to calcium; subscript 1 is not written.
Parentheses: two nitrate ions means (NO₃)₂.
Check: +2 + 2(−1) = 0.
Correct: Ca(NO₃)₂
Incorrect: CaNO₃₂ — this does not show two intact nitrate ions.
Shortcut boundary: Magic Cross helps set ionic subscripts. It does not apply to molecular compounds, where prefixes such as di-, tri-, and tetra- tell you the subscripts directly.
