Slide 2 • Counting Rules

Rules for Significant Figures (Sig Figs)

1. Non-Zero Digits

All non-zero integers are [_______]ALWAYS significant.

145.8 g → 4 Sig Figs
2. Captive (Trapped) Zeros

Zeros between non-zero digits are [_______]ALWAYS significant.

1.008 m → 4 Sig Figs
3. Leading Zeros

Zeros preceding all non-zero digits are [_______]NEVER significant (placeholders only).

0.0025 kg → 2 Sig Figs
4. Trailing Zeros

Trailing zeros in a number containing a decimal point are [_______]significant. Trailing zeros in a whole number without a displayed decimal point may be [_______]ambiguous. Use scientific notation to show the intended precision.

100.0 g (4 SF) vs. 100 g (Ambiguous; write 1.0 × 10² g for 2 SF)

Sig Figs in Addition & Subtraction

The Decimal Place Rule: The result must have the same number of [_______]decimal places as the measurement with the fewest decimal places.

Example Problem
  12.11  (2 decimal places)
+ 18.0   (1 decimal place ← FEWEST)
+ 1.013 (3 decimal places)
= 31.123 → 31.1
Key Distinction: Do NOT count total sig figs here! Only look at [_______]places past decimal.
Slide 5 • Multiplication & Division

Sig Figs in Multiplication & Division

The Total Sig Fig Rule: The result must contain the same number of [_______]total sig figs as the measurement with the fewest total sig figs.

Example Calculation
4.56 (3 SF) × 1.4 (2 SF ← FEWEST) = 6.384 → 6.4 (2 SF)
Exact Numbers: Counted objects (e.g., 3 apples) or defined conversion factors (1 in = 2.54 cm) have [_______]infinite sig figs and never limit calculations!
Slide 6 • Compact Representation

Scientific Notation (N × 10ⁿ)

Standard Scientific Form

N × 10n

Where 1 ≤ N < 10 and n is an integer exponent.

Eliminating Ambiguity

How do you write 300 with 2 Sig Figs? Standard decimal form is ambiguous!

300 with 2 SF = [_______]3.0 × 10²
Exponent Direction Quick Guide
Large Numbers (>10):
Move decimal left → Exponent is POSITIVE (+)
560000 → 5.6 × 10⁵
Small Numbers (<1):
Move decimal right → Exponent is NEGATIVE (-)
0.00045 → 4.5 × 10⁻⁴
Slide 7 • Combined Operations

Multi-Step Calculations & Rounding

Golden Rule of Rounding

At each operation, apply the correct significant-figure rule to determine that step’s precision limit. Carry all extra digits through [_______]intermediate steps as guard digits, and round ONLY the final reported answer.

Mixed Operations Sequence: (2.54 + 0.123) × 1.2
Step 1: 2.54 (2 dec) + 0.123 (3 dec) = 2.663 (calculator value; Step 1 is limited to the hundredths place → 2.66 if reported here; carry 2.663 forward as guard digits)
Step 2: use the unrounded 2.663 while carrying forward the Step 1 precision limit (3 SF for this value): 2.663 × 1.2 (2 SF) = 3.1956
Final Rounding (2 SF limit): 3.2

Slide 11 • Key Takeaways

Topic Summary — T1.3

1. Measuring & Uncertainty

Always record all certain digits plus 1 estimated digit.

2. Sig Fig Rules

Leading zeros are not significant. Trailing zeros in decimal numbers are significant, while trailing zeros in whole numbers may be ambiguous.

3. Operation Rules

Add/Sub = fewest decimal places. Mult/Div = fewest total sig figs.
