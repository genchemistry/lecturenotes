Periodic Classification — Effective Nuclear Charge

Learning Objective: Define Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff) and identify its overall direction on the periodic table.
Electron configuration → Periodic Classification

Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff)

The net nuclear attraction felt by an electron after the effects of other electrons are considered.

General trend

As proton number increases, Zeff generally becomes larger.

Color rule: red always refers to Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff) — the term, the arrow, and later trend references.

Shielding — Concept and Trend Map

Learning Objective: Explain shielding and identify its dominant direction on the periodic table.

Definition

Shielding is the reduction in nuclear attraction felt by an outer electron because inner electrons stand between that electron and the nucleus.

Main direction

Shielding increases down a group because more occupied shells are added.

Across a period, shielding changes much less.

Particle idea

When inner electrons lie between the nucleus and an outer electron, the outer electron feels less nuclear attraction.

Across a period, the added electrons enter the same principal shell, so same-shell electrons do not greatly change the shielding felt by the outer electron.

Penetration — How Close Can an Orbital Reach the Nucleus?

Learning Objective: Explain penetration qualitatively and connect it to shielding and orbital energy.

What does penetration mean?

Penetration describes how much electron density is found close to the nucleus.

Even within the same principal level, orbital shapes are not identical, so electrons in different subshells do not stay at the same average distance from the nucleus.

Same-n idea

s > p > d > f penetration

For the same value of n, an s orbital reaches closest to the nucleus, then p, then d, then f.

Key idea: a more penetrating orbital lets the electron spend more time close to the nucleus, so the electron feels less shielding and stronger attraction.

Why is penetration not on the trend map?

Penetration is not a simple left-right or top-bottom periodic-table trend of elements.

It is mainly a comparison of orbital types within an atom or within the same principal level.

That is why penetration is better discussed with orbital comparisons such as 3s, 3p, and 3d, rather than with a single periodic-table arrow.

What Controls Atomic Orbital Energy?

Learning Objective: Relate attraction, shielding, repulsion, and penetration to qualitative orbital-energy differences.

1. Stronger attraction to the nucleus = larger Zeff

A larger Zeff means the electron feels stronger nuclear attraction.

Stronger attraction makes the orbital energy lower.

larger Zeff / stronger attraction → lower energy

2. More shielding or more electron–electron repulsion

If the electron is more shielded or feels more repulsion from other electrons, its energy becomes higher.

more shielding / repulsion → higher energy

3. More penetration

More penetration means the electron can get closer to the nucleus, so the attraction is stronger and the energy becomes lower.

more penetration → lower energy

What does lower or higher energy mean?

Lower energy = more stable. The electron is held more tightly by the nucleus.

Higher energy = less stable. The electron is held less tightly and is easier to remove or excite.

Key comparison for the same n

for n = 3:   3s < 3p < 3d   energy

Because 3s penetrates more than 3p, and 3p more than 3d, the 3s orbital is lowest in energy among those three.

Bottom line: more attraction lowers orbital energy; more shielding or repulsion raises orbital energy.
This is the intuition behind why sublevels in many-electron atoms are not all at the same energy.
