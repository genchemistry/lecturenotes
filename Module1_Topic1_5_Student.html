Slide 2 • Physical Behavior

Physical Properties & Changes

Physical Property: Observed or measured without changing chemical [_______]identity (e.g., color, density, melting point).

Physical Change: A change in state or form that retains the [_______]same chemical substance (boiling, melting, grinding).

Slide 3 • Chemical Reactivity

Chemical Properties & Changes

Chemical Property: Ability of a substance to form [_______]new substances (e.g., flammability, acidity, toxicity).

Chemical Change: Produces one or more [_______]new substances with a different chemical composition (rusting, burning, digestion).

Possible Indicators of Chemical Change
  • Gas formation (bubbles produced without boiling).
  • Persistent color change (unexpected new color).
  • Energy change (temperature spike or light emission).
  • Formation of a precipitate (solid forming from solution).
  • Odor change — an unexpected new odor may indicate that new substances formed.
Slide 4 • Phase Transitions

Phase Changes (All Physical Changes)

Melting, Freezing & Vaporization

• Melting (Solid → Liquid)
• Freezing (Liquid → Solid)
• Vaporization/Boiling (Liquid → Gas)

Condensation, Sublimation & Deposition

• Condensation (Gas → Liquid)
• Sublimation (Solid → Gas, e.g., Dry Ice)
• Deposition (Gas → Solid, e.g., Frost)

Phase Transition Map
All phase transitions are physical changes because the chemical identity does not change!

Intensive vs. Extensive Properties

[_______]Intensive Properties

Independent of the amount of substance present. Excellent for identification.

Examples: Temperature, Density, Color, Boiling Point
[_______]Extensive Properties

Depends directly on the amount of matter in the sample.

Examples: Mass, Volume, Total Heat Content

Slide 8 • Common Traps

Frequent Student Misconceptions — T1.5

Mistake 1: Thinking dissolving sugar is [_______]chemical because it "disappears."

Fact: Dissolving is physical. Sugar molecules remain intact in solution and can be recovered by evaporating the water.

Mistake 2: Thinking boiling is chemical because it makes [_______]gas/bubbles.

Fact: The chemical identity remains H₂O; no new substance is formed.
