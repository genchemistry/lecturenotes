Percent Composition, Empirical Formula, and Molecular Formula — Big Picture

Learning Objective: Distinguish percent composition, empirical formula, and molecular formula and identify the pathway for each.
T3.1 connection: Molar mass gives the total mass of one mole. T3.2 asks how that total mass is divided among elements, how composition data reveal the simplest atom ratio, and how molar mass connects an empirical formula to a molecular formula.

Percent Composition

Formula → element mass contribution → total molar mass → percent

Empirical Formula

Mass or percent → moles → divide by smallest → whole-number ratio

Molecular Formula

Empirical formula → empirical-formula mass → compare with molar mass → multiply all subscripts

Before calculating: decide whether the problem asks for a percent, the simplest whole-number ratio, or the actual molecular formula.
This Topic includes percent-composition, empirical-formula, and molecular-formula practice before the final check.

Empirical Formula Workflow — Four Moves

Learning Objective: Apply a consistent mass-to-mole-to-ratio sequence.
1. Mass

Use grams; if percentages are given, assume 100 g.

2. Moles

Divide by each element’s molar mass.

3. Normalize

Divide all values by the smallest.

4. Whole Numbers

Multiply every ratio only when needed.

Percentages become grams only because a 100 g sample is assumed.

Never Use

40.0 : 6.7 : 53.3 directly as subscripts

Always Use

mole ratios as the basis for subscripts

Rounding rule: 1.99 → 2 is acceptable. 1.50 → 2 is not; multiply all ratios by 2.

Molecular Formula — Scale the Empirical Formula

Learning Objective: Determine a molecular formula from an empirical formula and the compound molar mass.

Empirical Formula

Simplest whole-number atom ratio.

Example: P₂O₅

Molecular Formula

Actual whole-number multiple of the empirical formula.

Molecular formula = n × empirical formula
Key idea: Every empirical-formula subscript must be multiplied by the same whole-number value of n.
n = molecular molar massempirical-formula mass
1. Find EF mass

P₂O₅ = 2(30.97) + 5(16.00) = 141.94 g/mol

2. Compare masses

283.88 g/mol141.94 g/mol = 2.00

3. Identify n

n = 2

4. Scale subscripts

P₂O₅ × 2 → P₄O₁₀

Molecular formula = [ _______ ]
Check: The molecular formula must be a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula. Its molar mass should match the given molecular molar mass.
Do not change only one subscript. The same n multiplies every subscript in the empirical formula.
