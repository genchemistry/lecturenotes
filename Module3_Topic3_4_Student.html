Limiting and Excess Reactants — Which Reactant Runs Out First?

Learning Objective: Explain limiting and excess reactants before calculation and connect the limiting reactant to theoretical yield
T3.3 connection: Stoichiometry calculated product from one given amount. T3.4 gives two reactant amounts, so we must determine which reactant controls the maximum product and which remains in excess.
2A+B→A₂B
Available A
3.0 mol A
Available B
5.0 mol B
Question
How much A₂B?
Recipe idea: 1 mol A₂B requires 2 mol A and 1 mol B.

A path

3.0 mol A × 1 mol A₂B2 mol A = 1.5 mol A₂B

B path

5.0 mol B × 1 mol A₂B1 mol B = 5.0 mol A₂B
A is the limiting reactant; product formed = 1.5 mol A₂B.
Excess B: 1.5 mol B is used, so 5.0 − 1.5 = 3.5 mol B remains.
Calculation rule: convert each reactant to the same product amount. The reactant that makes less product is limiting; that smaller product amount is the theoretical yield.

Excess Reactant Left — Convert Product Difference Back

Learning Objective: Find the mass of excess reactant left in grams using product amounts from the limiting-reactant comparison.
ACS-Style Follow-Up
For 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO, 10.0 g Mg and 10.0 g O₂ react. After identifying the limiting reactant, how many grams of the excess reactant remain?
Find the leftover excess reactant in g.
Return to the mass-given worked example: We already compared both reactant paths as product amounts in moles.

Mg path

Mg can form 0.411 mol MgO.

Actual product: 0.411 mol MgO

O₂ path

O₂ could form 0.625 mol MgO.

O₂ is excess.

Product difference = product that could have been made by leftover O₂

0.625 mol MgO − 0.411 mol MgO = 0.214 mol MgO

This difference belongs to the excess path. It represents the MgO that leftover O₂ could have made if more Mg had been available.

0.214 mol MgO × 1 mol O₂ 2 mol MgO × 32.00 g O₂ 1 mol O₂
mol MgO → mol O₂ → g O₂
Excess O₂ left = [ _______ ]
O₂ used ≈ 6.58 g
O₂ left ≈ 3.42 g

Percent Yield — Actual vs Theoretical Product

Learning Objective: Calculate percent yield by comparing actual and theoretical amounts of the same product
% yield = actual yield of producttheoretical yield of product × 100

Theoretical Yield

Maximum product predicted from stoichiometry and the limiting reactant.

Actual Yield

Amount of that same product obtained experimentally.

Product-only rule: percent yield compares actual and theoretical amounts of the same product. Do not put reactant mass directly into the percent-yield fraction. Reactant amounts matter only when they are first used to calculate theoretical product yield.
Theoretical MgO
16.6 g
Actual MgO
14.9 g
% yield = 14.9 g MgO16.6 g MgO × 100
Percent yield = [ _______ ]
Check: actual is slightly smaller than theoretical, so percent yield should be below 100%.
