Worked Problems — Radioactivity (Easy → Medium → Hard)

Learning Objective: Solve introductory ACS-style radioactivity problems by tracking mass number A and atomic number Z.

Easy

Question: A nucleus emits an α particle. How do the mass number A and atomic number Z change?

Step 1: An α particle is 2 protons + 2 neutrons.

Step 2: Losing 2 protons and 2 neutrons means losing 4 total mass units and 2 positive charges.

Answer: A decreases by 4 and Z decreases by 2.

Medium

Question: Carbon-14 undergoes β⁻ decay. What are the mass number and atomic number of the daughter nucleus?

Step 1: Carbon-14 has A = 14 and carbon has Z = 6.

Step 2: In β⁻ decay, a neutron becomes a proton and an electron is emitted.

Step 3: So A stays the same, but Z increases by 1.

Answer: Daughter nucleus = A = 14, Z = 7 (nitrogen-14).

Hard

Question: A parent nucleus forms a daughter nucleus whose mass number is 4 lower and atomic number is 2 lower. What was emitted?

Step 1: Compare the changes: ΔA = −4 and ΔZ = −2.

Step 2: Match that pattern with the three emissions:

  • α: A − 4, Z − 2
  • β⁻: A no change, Z + 1
  • γ: A no change, Z no change
Answer: The nucleus emitted an α particle.
