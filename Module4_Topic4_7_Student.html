Dilution — Same Solute, More Solvent

Learning Objective: Explain dilution visually and connect it to conservation of moles of solute.

Before dilution — stock solution

smaller volume, higher concentration

+ H₂O →

After dilution

larger volume, lower concentration

What changed? We added solvent, so volume increased and concentration decreased.
What did NOT change? No solute was added or removed, so the moles of solute stay the same.

Worked Dilution — Find the Final Volume

Learning Objective: Solve a dilution problem by labeling initial and final quantities before substituting.

Problem

A 1.67 M NaOH solution is prepared from 50.0 mL of 4.4 M NaOH. What is the final volume?

Solution 1M1 = 4.4 M
V1 = 50.0 mL
Solution 2M2 = 1.67 M
V2 = ?

Set up before arithmetic

M1V1 = M2V2
V2 = M1V1M2
V2 = (4.4 M)(50.0 mL)1.67 M
V2 = 1.3 × 10² mL
Reasonableness: concentration decreased, so the final volume must be larger than 50.0 mL.

Preparing a Dilute Solution from Stock

Learning Objective: Calculate the required stock volume and translate the answer into a laboratory preparation.

Problem

How would you prepare 450. mL of 0.27 M saline from a 1.40 M stock solution?

stock M1
find V1
transfer stock
add water
450. mL final

Calculation

M1V1 = M2V2
V1 = M2V2M1
V1 = (0.27 M)(450. mL)1.40 M = 87 mL

Prepare the diluted solution

Measure 87 mL of the 1.40 M stock solution.
Transfer it to a container used to make the final solution.
Add distilled water until the final solution volume is 450. mL.
Mix thoroughly.
Do not add 450. mL of water. The final solution volume must be 450. mL.

Worked Titration — Standardizing HCl with NaOH

Learning Objective: Use buret readings, molarity, and a 1:1 reaction to find an unknown acid concentration.

Given

20.00 mL HCl is titrated with 0.241 M NaOH.

Initial buret1.56 mL
Final buret21.87 mL
HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)

Step 1 — NaOH delivered → mol NaOH → mol HCl

V(NaOH) = 21.87 mL − 1.56 mL = 20.31 mL = 0.02031 L
0.02031 L × 0.241 mol NaOH1 L = 0.004895 mol NaOH
0.004895 mol NaOH × 1 mol HCl1 mol NaOH = 0.004895 mol HCl

Step 2 — mol HCl → molarity of HCl

M(HCl) = 0.004895 mol HCl0.02000 L HCl = 0.245 M HCl
Answer: 0.245 M HCl

Gravimetric Analysis — Measure an Unknown by Weighing a Precipitate

Learning Objective: Explain the basic gravimetric-analysis workflow and connect precipitate mass to the amount of analyte.
Gravimetric analysis: convert the substance being measured into a solid precipitate, isolate the solid, dry it, and weigh it.
1. React
form an insoluble precipitate
2. Filter
separate solid from solution
3. Dry
remove water
4. Weigh
mass precipitate
5. Calculate
mass → mol → analyte

Precipitation

aqueous sample + reagent → solid

Dry & weigh

use stoichiometry to work backward to the unknown

Why dry the precipitate? Water would add extra mass and make the calculated amount of analyte too large.

Worked Gravimetric Analysis — Chloride from AgCl

Learning Objective: Determine chloride concentration from the mass of AgCl precipitate.

Problem

A 50.0 mL sample containing Cl⁻ produces 0.287 g AgCl after excess AgNO₃ is added. Find [Cl⁻].

Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s)
g AgCl
mol AgCl
mol Cl⁻
L sample
M Cl⁻

Step 1 — precipitate mass → mol Cl⁻

Molar mass AgCl = 143.32 g per mol
0.287 g AgCl × 1 mol AgCl143.32 g AgCl = 0.00200 mol AgCl
0.00200 mol AgCl × 1 mol Cl⁻1 mol AgCl = 0.00200 mol Cl⁻

Step 2 — mol Cl⁻ → concentration

50.0 mL = 0.0500 L
[Cl⁻] = 0.00200 mol Cl⁻0.0500 L = 0.0400 M Cl⁻
Answer: 0.0400 M Cl⁻

A. 0.0750 M   B. 0.100 M   C. 0.120 M   D. 0.300 M

Answer: C. 0.03000 L × 0.100 mol NaOH1 L = 0.00300 mol NaOH = 0.00300 mol HCl; M = 0.00300 mol0.02500 L = 0.120 M.

Hard — Gravimetric Analysis

A chloride sample gives 0.143 g AgCl. Which first calculation is correct?

A. multiply 0.143 g by 143.32 g per mol
B. divide 0.143 g by 143.32 g per mol
C. divide 143.32 g by 0.143 g
D. multiply by the sample volume first

Answer: B. First convert measured precipitate mass to moles: 0.143 g AgCl × 1 mol AgCl143.32 g AgCl.
