Isotopes: Atoms of the same element with the same number of [_______] but different numbers of [_______].

Isotopes have the same atomic number but different mass numbers.

Their chemical behavior is generally similar because neutral isotopes have the same electron count.

Mass Number, Isotope Mass, and Average Atomic Mass

Learning Objective: Distinguish mass number, measured isotope mass, and periodic-table average atomic mass.
QuantityMeaningExample
Mass number Whole-number count of p⁺ + n⁰ for one isotope. C-12 → 12
Isotope mass Experimentally measured mass of one isotope in [_______]. C-12 → 12.0000 amu
Average atomic mass Abundance-weighted average of naturally occurring isotopes. Carbon → 12.011 amu
These three numbers are related but are not interchangeable.

Natural Isotopic Abundance

Learning Objective: Convert percent abundance to fractional abundance and check abundance totals.

100-Atom Sample

Natural abundance: Percentage of atoms in a natural sample that are a particular isotope.

70 atoms of isotope A; 30 atoms of isotope B

Percent Abundance

Isotope A = 70%

Isotope B = 30%

Percent abundances sum to [_______]%.

Fractional Abundance

70% ÷ 100 = 0.70

30% ÷ 100 = 0.30

Fractional abundances sum to [_______].

Convert percent to fraction: divide by 100.

Guided Calculation — Unknown Isotopic Abundance with x and 1 − x

Learning Objective: Solve a two-isotope abundance problem by representing the two fractional abundances as x and 1 − x.

Given — Nitrogen Isotopes

Nitrogen has two naturally occurring isotopes. Its average atomic mass is 14.007 amu.

IsotopeIsotope MassFractional Abundance
N-1414.00374 amux
N-1515.000108 amu1 − x
Why 1 − x? The two fractional abundances must add to 1.000.

Set Up and Solve

14.007 = (14.00374)(x) + (15.000108)(1 − x)

14.007 = 14.00374x + 15.000108 − 15.000108x

14.007 = 15.000108 − 0.996368x

0.993108 = 0.996368x

x = 0.996728

N-14 Abundance

x = 0.996728

N-14 = [_______]

N-15 Abundance

1 − x = 1 − 0.996728 = 0.003272

N-15 = [_______]

Check: 99.67% + 0.33% = 100.00%. The average, 14.007 amu, is much closer to 14.00374 amu, so N-14 should be much more abundant.
For exactly two isotopes, using x and 1 − x automatically enforces the requirement that the two fractional abundances sum to 1.
