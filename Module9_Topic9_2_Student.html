Lattice Energy: Separating an Ionic Crystal

Learning Objective: Describe lattice energy using a clearly stated physical process.
Lattice energy tells us how much energy is needed to separate one mole of an ionic solid into its gaseous ions.
NaCl(s) → Na⁺(g) + Cl⁻(g)
Separating the ions requires energy. We use this positive separation-energy convention for comparisons.
Na⁺(g) + Cl⁻(g) → NaCl(s)
The reverse process forms the crystal and releases the same amount of energy. State the process before deciding the sign.

What Makes Ionic Attraction Stronger?

Learning Objective: Compare charge and ion size without using a proportionality formula.
Effect of chargeEffect of ion size
Larger charge magnitudes strengthen electrostatic attraction when other factors are comparable.With the same charges, smaller ions can approach more closely and attract more strongly.
Example: MgO (Mg²⁺, O²⁻) has a much larger lattice separation energy than NaF (Na⁺, F⁻).Example: NaF has a larger lattice separation energy than NaCl because F⁻ is smaller than Cl⁻.
Charge does not determine distance by itself. Check both ionic charge and ionic size; do not assume that every ±2 pair must be closer than every ±1 pair.

Breaking and Forming Bonds Have Opposite Signs

Learning Objective: Distinguish energy absorbed in bond breaking from energy released in bond formation.
ProcessEnergy transferSign
Break a bondEnergy inputPositive
Form that bond from the same separated atomsEnergy releaseNegative
A combustion reaction can release energy overall even though its first bond-breaking steps require energy. Compare the full energy input with the full energy release.

A Calculation Path Before Arithmetic

Learning Objective: Estimate reaction enthalpy from a complete bond inventory.
Balance + draw structures→Count bonds→Calculate input and release→Subtract
ΔHrxn ≈ ΣD(bonds broken) − ΣD(bonds formed)
D values are positive dissociation energies. Subtract the positive total for bonds formed; do not enter a negative formation total and subtract it again.
Average bond enthalpies estimate gas-phase reactions. States matter when comparing with a measured reaction enthalpy. The complete bond inventory is a Hess-law bookkeeping path, not a sequence in which every reactant bond must break before any product bond forms.

ΔH ≈ Σ(n × D) for bonds broken − Σ(n × D) for bonds formed
Multiply each bond count by its bond energy. Add the reactant terms, then subtract the product terms.
ΔH ≈ [5(413) + 348 + 358 + 463 + 3(495)] − [4(799) + 6(463)]
Energy to break bonds = 4719 kJ; energy released by forming bonds = 5974 kJ
ΔH ≈ 4719 − 5974 = [ _______ ]
Negative ΔH: forming product bonds releases more energy than breaking reactant bonds requires. The reaction is exothermic.

Average bond enthalpies estimate the gas-phase reaction shown. For liquid ethanol with the same gaseous products, add its positive vaporization enthalpy; water condensation would require a separate correction.
