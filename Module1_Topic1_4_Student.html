Density & Intensive Properties

Density is defined as [_______]mass per unit volume of a substance.

Density (D) = mass (m) / volume (V)

Intensive vs. Extensive Properties

Density is an [_______]intensive physical property. It does NOT depend on the quantity of matter present.

By contrast, mass and volume are [_______]extensive properties because their values change as sample size changes.

D = m / V

Use when given mass and volume to find density directly.

Multiply density by volume. Volume units [_______]cancel out leaving mass.

Solving for Volume
V = m / D

Slide 3 • Exam Shortcut

The Density Triangle Method

Density Magic Triangle
Cover the variable you want to solve for!

How to Use the Triangle

  • Cover m: You see D side-by-side with V → m = D × V
  • Cover D: You see m over V → D = m / V
  • Cover V: You see m over D → V = m / D
Important Warning: While the triangle is a handy memory tool, always verify unit cancellation with dimensional analysis to avoid exam traps!
Slide 4 • Units & Conversions

Unit Consistency & Core Equivalencies

The Critical Volume Equivalency

1 mL = 1 cm³ = 1 cc

One milliliter (1 mL) of liquid volume is [_______]identical in volume to a cube measuring 1 cm × 1 cm × 1 cm (1 cm³).

Solids
g/cm³
e.g. Iron = 7.87 g/cm³
Liquids
g/mL
e.g. Water = 1.00 g/mL
Gases
g/L
e.g. Oxygen = 1.43 g/L
Unit Matching Rule: Mass and volume units MUST match the units of density before plugging numbers into your calculation!
Slide 5 • Guided Example

Water Displacement Method (Rebar)

Problem Statement

A piece of rebar with mass 69.658 g is dropped into a graduated cylinder containing water. The initial water level is 13.5 mL and the final level is 22.4 mL. Calculate the density of the rebar.

Step 1: Calculate Displaced Volume
Vdisplaced = Vfinal − Vinitial = 22.4 mL − 13.5 mL = [_______]8.9 mL (1 dec place → 2 SF)
Step 2: Calculate Density with Sig Figs
D = m / V = 69.658 g (5 SF) / 8.9 mL (2 SF) = 7.82674... g/mL
Final Rounded Density = [_______]7.8 g/mL (2 SF)

Slide 6 • Guided Example

Solving for Mass (Gold Bar)

Problem Statement

Gold has a density of 19.3 g/cm³. What is the mass in grams of a gold bar with a volume of 4.50 × 10² mL?

Step 1: Check Unit Compatibility

Since 1 mL = 1 cm³, the volume is 4.50 × 10² cm³. The volume units match density!

Step 2: Formula & Calculation
m = D × V
m = (19.3 g/cm³) × (4.50 × 10² cm³)
m = 8.685 × 10³ g
Final Mass (3 SF) = [_______]8.69 × 10³ g (or 8690 g)

Solving for Volume (Ethanol Sample)

Problem Statement

Ethanol has a density of 0.789 g/mL. What volume in mL is occupied by a 25.0 g sample of ethanol?

Step 1: Set Up Formula
V = m / D = 25.0 g / 0.789 g/mL
Step 2: Calculate & Apply Sig Figs
Calculator output: 31.685678... mL

Slide 8 • Interpretation

Comparing & Interpreting Density

Floating vs. Sinking Principle

An object or liquid will float in a liquid if its density is [_______]less than that of the liquid. It will sink if its density is [_______]greater than the liquid.

The Water Anomaly (Ice Floating)

Unlike most substances, liquid water expands upon freezing!

• Ice (0°C): 0.917 g/cm³
• Liquid Water (4°C): 1.000 g/cm³
→ Result: Ice floats, preserving aquatic life beneath frozen lakes!
Density Column Layering
Least dense on top → Most dense on bottom
Slide 9 • Common Traps

Frequent Student Mistakes — Topic T1.4

Mistake 1: Inverting the density formula when solving for volume.

Correction: Volume is V = m / D, NOT D / m! Always verify with unit cancellation.

Mistake 2: Mismatched volume units (1 L vs 1 mL).

Correction: Convert liters to milliliters (1 L = 1000 mL) before multiplying or dividing by density in g/mL.

Mistake 3: Forgetting subtraction rules for water displacement.

Correction: Determine sig figs of displaced volume using [_______]decimal places before dividing mass by volume!
